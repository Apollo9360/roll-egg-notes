# Core loop design - Structure

Up: [[Core loop design]] · Next: [[Core loop design - Moment-to-moment feel]]

- Focus on one simple loop: action, challenge, reward.
- Keep it to about four tasks or fewer. Complex loops can work for some genres and demographics.
- Pick one main idea. Stacking buzzwords or combining several genres (incremental plus RNG plus action) confuses players.
- A five-game mashup got 1.1% CTR and 96% bounce. Mixing known mechanics in one coherent way (keyboard plus ASMR, or autonomous sensory meridian response, plus speed obby) can work.
- Do not mix RNG (random number generation) and incremental designs:
    - RNG: idle generation plus inventory; kept mutated items generate cash.
    - Incremental: grow by collecting around an open map.
- RNG elements do not have to define a game; adding RNG pieces is fine.
- Do not mix a slow, relaxing cleaning loop with a fast +1 speed loop; +1 players want low-input speed and upgrades.
- A minority view: linked loops add depth, such as mining plus a furnace that refines ore.
- Cut steps. One merge game went from "buy, collect babies, deposit, collect cash" to "merge, earn, buy more".
- An enjoyable game already has a good loop. Design then means progression that makes it sticky, tuned by iterating on stats.
- Find the boring pain point in a loop and make it far more fun. One game took Oil Empire's spot by redesigning selling.
- Loop-based games beat mini-game and party games, which often neglect the loop.
- Make PvE (player versus environment) the main mode. Add PvP (player versus player) later as a separate arena.
- Simulators should be something you can progress in constantly. Grow a Garden had offline growth; Steal a Brainrot had stealing.
- Steal a Brainrot worked because stealing lets you take someone else's lucky RNG result.
- Design is iterative: build a mechanic, play it, change it. Cut features that hurt the game regardless of sunk cost.
