# Saturation, clones and reskins

Up: [[Game Design, Genres and Live Ops]] · Previous: [[Genre benchmarks]] · Next: [[Designing for kids, audiences and naming]]

- Most Roblox games are copies or inspired by others. Competition shows the market is alive.
- Pure reskins usually fail. Make a mechanical twist that alters the core loop (owner-recommended).
- Reskins that drop the core system fail. +1 Mine per Click's 30k CCU came from item drops plus selling; a clone without them flopped.
- Adding flavors to a generator-plus-storage loop is not enough (Snowcone Stand stalled at 1.5k CCU).
- Do not copy a hit late: kids recognize copies and the leader holds the market. Capture players leaving a declining hit with a real alternative instead.
- Avoid launching close to a popular game, even a 5k-CCU one. Raise a Horse (10k) and Horse RNG (5k) blocked a later horse game.
- Fast copies often beat originals. A brainrot betting copy reached 50k while the original sat at 400; a 1:1 tycoon replica reached 17–20k.
- Copy-and-improve: copy a game with high visits but poor balance, remake it properly and outspend it on ads.
- Copying a 10k-CCU game typically yields 1–10k; copying a 1k-CCU loop yields about 100. Copying a 1M-CCU game will not yield 100k+.

### Templates

- Templates are disputed (disputed):
    - Against (owner-recommended): they monetize poorly, rarely enter the algorithm, and players prefer the original. One developer wasted 200k+ Robux on ads across 10+ templates.
    - For: an unchanged $15 template hit 7k CCU in a month. One team shipped 2–3 same-code games in a week at 1k+ each.
- If you use one, treat it as a starting point, not the ceiling, and change the map if heavily modified.
- Templates cost about 5k Robux; cheap one-tap FPS templates cost about $80. Competing with Rivals-level FPS needs about $10k USD.
- Stud-style model packs are commonly bought; one animal and egg pack cost about 15k Robux.

### Cash-grab versus evergreen

- A cash-grab ("slop") game is packed with microtransactions for quick money; players dislike it, but it can pay.
- "Slop" is often a label for anything that does well. Bad execution (play-through rate, thumbnails, update cadence) is usually the real problem.
- Use cash-grabs as stepping stones: stack cash, then fund a longer-term project (owner-recommended).
- Evergreen games rarely profit in the first 6 months; expect about a year. Investors tend to avoid them for slow return on investment (ROI).
- A well-monetized game holding 300 CCU can pay $1,000+ a month for years. A kid-focused 2k-CCU game is enough to live on.
- Portfolio model: 20–40 small games at 50–300 CCU each, but each needs updates and ads and a dedicated team.
- Some ship a game a day and update whichever survives. One developer lost 650k Robux over 13 games before every release hit 5k+.
- Always have another game in the pipeline when one dies (owner-recommended).

### Timing

- Ship within about 3 days of a new trend; early copies win. Trend games die with the trend unless updated.
- A rushed trend leader leaves room for a better version to take its players.
- If your idea exists and is done better, reuse assets and flip the loop instead of polishing a clone.
- Most advise against posting ideas publicly. Novel games are harder to copy well, so copycats worry the owner less.
- Copyrighted IP (intellectual property) in a title will likely draw a strike. Avoid brand names for items too.
