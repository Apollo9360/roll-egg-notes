# What makes a hit

Up: [[Game Design, Genres and Live Ops]] · Next: [[Finding and validating ideas]]

- A simple game reaching 60k CCU in a month usually has:
    - No identical game yet, but familiar systems combined (a Slime RNG-style skill tree, a 99 Nights or Dead Rails-style lobby).
    - A loop driven by curiosity: what comes next, what is at the end.
    - Excellent polish, plus some luck.
- The winning formula is a strong fantasy plus a unique twist on a saturated idea (owner-recommended).
- No core loop is truly new; it has existed somewhere. Take a proven core loop and remix it (owner-recommended).
- Execution matters as much as the idea. A copy can reach 10k CCU while the original sits at 400.
- The idea still sets the ceiling. The gap between 1k and 100k CCU is usually the idea; a poor concept gets no CCU regardless of quality.
- Theme packaging often matters more than mechanics. Steal an Egg barely differs from tsunami-brainrot or kick-a-lucky-block games; the eggs made the difference.
- Theme alone can decide results: a soccer-card bidding game hit 2.4k CCU instantly while a near-identical lucky-block version had 0.
- First of a kind wins: Clean the Squishies reached 30k CCU as the first of its type.
- A twist must be novel but easy to grasp, like Build a Ring Farm's rotating crop-harvesting blade.
- You should be able to explain the game in one sentence. Players should know what to do within about 5 seconds.
- About 80% of Roblox is mobile. Every breakout game pairs great mobile UX (user experience) with simplicity: depth without complexity (owner-recommended).
- Core loop is king: games with crude user interfaces (UI), default thumbnails or AI-made UI have still reached 20k–1M+ CCU.
- The player's view beats the developer's. Young developers making games for kids have an edge.
- Nobody can reliably predict a genre's CCU. Identical games can get wildly different results, so luck is a real factor.
- Roblox income is volatile; do not depend on it for essentials like tuition.
- Many recent hits were fully AI-coded, but they still needed systems planning, creativity and bug-testing. One-prompt clones often sit at 0 CCU.
- AI-coded games often validate remote events on the client and get exploited.
