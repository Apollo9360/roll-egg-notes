# Designing for kids, audiences and naming

Up: [[Game Design, Genres and Live Ops]] · Previous: [[Saturation, clones and reskins]] · Next: [[Polish, juice and visual style]]

### Kid legibility

- Platform demographics are about 60–80% kids, with roughly 60% under 16.
- Audience tiers: ages 5–8 who barely read, mid-age readers who retain poorly, and older players who give feedback.
- Kids prefer simple, colorful UI from one palette with consistent padding.
- UI quality matters less for young audiences: Sell Lemons passed 100k CCU with about 112 buttons. Some argue UI works subconsciously (disputed).
- Use neon red for damage parts; kids know it means danger.
- Unfamiliar terms lose kids; a spleef game failed for that reason.
- "UGC" (user-generated content) in a title draws kids expecting real UGC, who then dislike the game.
- Hide unneeded UI during onboarding. Make all UI mobile-friendly.

### Mobile and controls

- Mobile readability is the top UX concern; every point of friction matters (owner-recommended).
- Fast characters are hard to control on mobile. Slow speed boosts on mobile.
- Put cash and rebirth buttons where the mobile thumb rests.
- Put the timer top-center, show "Checkpoint reached" notices, and fix confusing level boundaries.
- Show the control scheme in the HUD (heads-up display) for console and PC players.
- Console is about 3% of players, between PC and mobile on playtime and spend. Enabling it improved CCU, retention and revenue in reports.

### Other audiences

- Female players are underserved. Decorating, customization and gardening carry many top games; one test saw women skip PvP entirely.
- Cleaning and cafe games run about 80% female; one incremental showed about 60%.
- Incrementals, tycoon RNG and Miner's Haven-style games skew older, often 18+, which caps CCU but raises spend.
- Older players tolerate less friction: add a saturation toggle and drop random group-join prompts.
- Older players may leave over AI art or poor UI. They are less contested but hard to reach without hype.
- Hangout games are probably the biggest 17+ genre (low confidence).
- The biggest wins appeal to kids and adults together; 99 Nights and Grow a Garden drew parents too.

### Naming

- "Verb a Noun" titles work because players think in experiences: Grow a Garden, Steal a Brainrot, Tame a Pet.
- Shorter usually wins: "Merge Animals" beats "Animal Farm: Collect & Steal". Some top niche games use long names (disputed).
- "+1 Train" beats "Train Simulator" while +1 naming trends. "Simulator" naming is largely dead.
- RNG games still work when titled "RNG". Whether to put "incremental" in the name is disputed.
- Weak "[verb] [noun] incremental" names lose to themes like "noobs" or "studs".
- Use simple tiered item names ("copper GPU"), not brand-like names.
- Once a game passes about 100 CCU, avoid changing the icon; title changes are riskier above about 1k.
