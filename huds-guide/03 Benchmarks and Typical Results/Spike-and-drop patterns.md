# Spike-and-drop patterns

Up: [[Benchmarks and Typical Results]] · Previous: [[Events and growth paths]] · Next: [[Retention, playtime and funnel patterns]]

| Pattern | Typical course | Range seen | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Post-ad collapse | Falls to under 10% of ad CCU within a day | 200 to ~10; 636 to 6; 1k to 15 the next day | High |
| Recs drained despite green stats | Falls 80–95% over hours to days | 350 to 40 at top-percentile QPTR as impressions fell from 20k/hour to 400 | High |
| Post-peak settling | Settles far below the peak | 2k on launch ads to ~400; 1.8k peak to 300–500 two weeks later | High |
| Trend decay | Large games lose 80–99% | 60k to 1k; 1M to 500; 25k to 500; 1.2M to ~20k in 2 weeks | High |
| Bad update | Can halve or end a game | 20k to 7k after a rushed update by an unvetted developer (owner); a patch cut D1 from 20% to 10% and CCU from 20k to 11k | High |
| Bugs, bans and outages | Can kill a game outright | 2.7k lost to a competitor after a buggy update; 80 CCU never recovered after a 10-day ban | Medium |
| Seasonal shifts | Moderate drops with no change | D1 fell from 20% to 15% when school started | Medium |
| Stale content | Slow decline | Momentum lost after 2 weeks without updates; some games held 800 CCU for months | Medium |

- "Slop" trend games often die within a month; long-lived games like tower or roleplay classics hold for years.
- A leading niche game may hold ~600 CCU for months until a better competitor launches.
