# How thresholds and rules shifted over time

Up: [[Benchmarks and Typical Results]] · Previous: [[Target benchmarks]] · Next: [[Ad spend and early CCU outcomes]]

Most experienced developers treat 3%+ ad CTR as good. A minority used looser scales, calling 2.3%+ fair or noting that big creators sit at 1–2%.

| Period (2026) | Ad CTR guidance | Rule and platform changes |
|---|---|---|
| Late May to June | ~3% good; 3.8% on a large sample called excellent by the owner | HEP quota launched at 500, with a 100k-Robux expedited-review fee. A mid-June update cut ad-driven CCU sharply; one $20/day campaign fell from 70–100 CCU to 2–5. |
| Early July | 2.5–4% normal; ~5.5% good; 1% means stop | Tutorial target 70%+. Algorithm changes on July 1 and 4 cut one game's playtime from 32 to 23 min and PCR from 8% to 3%. |
| Mid to late July | Under 2% below average; 3% minimum; 4–5%+ good | Impressions per credit rose from ~11k in June to as high as 33k. |
| Early August | ~2.8% average; 4–5% exceptional | Tutorial target raised to ~90%. |
| Mid August | 2.7%+ good; most campaigns end around 2–3% | HEP quota cut from 500 to 250 from about August 19. |
| Late August | 4% minimum; under 0.5% hopeless | Tutorial target settled at 80%+. A bot-ban wave lowered CCU across many games. |
| September | 3–4% minimum; 1.5–4% average; top developers want 8%+ | Expedited fee cut from 100k to 50k Robux around mid-September. Algorithm swings around September 25 sent some games from thousands of CCU to near zero. |
| October | Above 3% good; ~1.5–2% average; over 5% excellent | Quota still 250. Several advertisers saw impressions per credit collapse. HEP accrual seemed slower (~30/day before, 11–12/day after). |
| November (announced) |  | HEP quota due to drop to 100. A 25k-Robux fee is an unverified rumor. |
