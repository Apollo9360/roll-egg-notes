# Discord and Roblox group communities

Up: [[Marketing, Teams and Business]] · Previous: [[Paying creators and clippers]] · Next: [[Personal brand and networking]]

- Build a Discord around your game so you own the traffic (owner-recommended). Start during development.
- Even ~170 members helped one game grow from 20 to 1.3k CCU with no ads. About 230 members is a good start.
- Big launches bring big communities. Anime games launch with 100k-member Discords; one hit drew 30k launch CCU from 100k members.
- Discord size does not equal CCU. 5k members held a game under 100 CCU; 13k gave 47 active players.
- A game at 1–5k CCU with only ~500 Discord members is probably living on ads.
- Young players, such as those of farming hits, rarely use Discord. They find games through recommendations and Shorts.
- One studio-branded server for all your games eases testing (low confidence).
- For validation, join the Discords of the biggest similar games rather than general chats.
- Add your Discord through the game's Social Links. Accounts under 16 cannot add social links.
- A game-bio link reportedly crashed CCU by about 90% in some games (low confidence).
- A visible Discord attracts scouts and studios to your DMs (direct messages) from about 5 CCU.
- Handle Discord raids with a moderation bot that bans by user ID. Pause invites and delete new invite links.
- Use in-game UI such as "join our Discord for codes" to move players across.
- In large Roblox groups, only about 1% of members see announcements (300k members gives ~3k impressions).
- Group update notifications brought about +15 CCU in one case.
- Group join prompts with rewards convert well. One game saw 1 join per 38 visits, roughly 1 in 5 unique players.
- Group joins do not bring likes. One prompt gave 160 members on day 1 and no likes.
- A big group is not enough alone. A 25k-member group never produced a 1k CCU game.
- Group members rarely bring players to a new game of a different genre. Discord members do help.
- Excessive like, favorite, group and social prompts look spammy.
- Group member demographics show your audience's gender split.
- A developer-focused audience tends to join briefly and leave. Most consider the bounce-rate effect negligible.
