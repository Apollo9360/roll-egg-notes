# Paying creators and clippers

Up: [[Marketing, Teams and Business]] · Previous: [[Short-form video playbook]] · Next: [[Discord and Roblox group communities]]

| Service | Typical price | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Small or young creator video | 500–1k Robux per video | Medium |
| Larger creator video | 30k–50k Robux per video | Low |
| Cross-posting service | About $10 per video on 3 platforms | Low |
| Trailer or clip edit | $10–20 for a 25–90 second edit | Medium |
| Community or content marketing manager | About $1k per month | Low |
| Clipper on revenue share | 10%; one got 500 views and quit | Low |

- Contact small-to-medium creators in your niche. Niche games, such as puzzle games, can do better with niche YouTubers than ads.
- Micro-influencers with 4–5k followers and niche TikTok live streamers have helped.
- Some small channels offer coverage for a revenue share. One very large creator quoted only $250 (low confidence).
- Tower defense games mostly use paid YouTuber placements.
- To raise the like ratio, ask small Twitch streamers to like your game.
- Paid TikTok cost-per-acquisition ads were suggested but are untested (low confidence).
- Finding TikTokers for paid promos has no reliable route. Some promo sellers take payment and deliver nothing.
- A guerrilla tactic: invite players in public servers of similar-genre games. One developer reported hundreds of uniques (low confidence).
