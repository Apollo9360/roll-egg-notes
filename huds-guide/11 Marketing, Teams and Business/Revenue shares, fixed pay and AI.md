# Revenue shares, fixed pay and AI

Up: [[Marketing, Teams and Business]] · Previous: [[Commission rates and asset prices]] · Next: [[Investors and publishers]]

- Solo developers with no money should do commissions first, then hire per job (owner-recommended).
- Stack money as a solo developer, then fund a team for bigger games. Solo developers should run one game at a time.
- Keep teams to 2–3 people. Hires asking large percentages make 2–3 partners better than hiring.
- Do not pay contractors fully upfront. Full prepayment has led to weeks of delay and vanished contractors.
- Get contracts that assign IP (intellectual property) to you. A fired scripter could otherwise DMCA the game.

| Role or contribution | Typical share | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Builder or scripter on a small owner-led game | About 15% each | Medium |
| Lead role on a game with no other hires | 5–10%, more for the role the game centers on | Medium |
| Each developer when you pay many commissions | 2–5% | Medium |
| Partner coder, UI designer, investor | About 20%, 15%, 20% in one team | Medium |
| Game-design-only partner | 5–10% | Medium |
| Live ops | About 20–30% | High |
| Marketing-only partner | Never more than about 30% | Medium |
| Polish work | 5–10% | Low |

- A 30/70 split is considered fair for a partner.
- Do not give percentages to builders or modelers unless they are needed repeatedly for updates.
- A scripter took 5% equity in exchange for a 70% fee discount.
- Percentage-only recruitment posts are viewed sceptically; owners may hide the real revenue.
- Idea theft happens often. Finish about 50%, or an AI prototype, before approaching a partner.
- Pay enough that stealing is not worth it. Trust matters more than raw skill.
- Making a game with random people rarely works because of trust, revenue disputes and motivation.

### AI versus hiring

- Most developers use AI for some scripting, and that is fine. AI still needs a human operator.
- A common low-budget workflow: AI for scripting, cheap packs or commissions for models, plain UI, and a paid UI rework only on success.
- A suggested one-game budget: about $100–150 for AI tools, $200 for ads and $100 contingency.
- AI plans vary: a $20 plan is enough for some, while heavy agent use exhausts $100–200 plans fast.
- You cannot vibe-code well without understanding scripting and systems. Several paid "programmers" turned out to be AI-only.
- AI is weak for creative UI, VFX and animation. Outsource those first once you earn money.
- AI works for vector icons, repetitive parts and models from a commissioned style.
    - Commission a few models, then feed the style to AI for the rest.
    - AI 3D models break down for car-customization games.
- AI versus hiring overall is disputed:
    - A minority see no need for developers below about $30k a month revenue.
    - Many argue AI is good for an MVP (minimum viable product) and nothing above it.
    - The server owner would use only AI to maximize profit, but prefers a passionate team.
