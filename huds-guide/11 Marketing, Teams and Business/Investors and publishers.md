# Investors and publishers

Up: [[Marketing, Teams and Business]] · Previous: [[Revenue shares, fixed pay and AI]] · Next: [[Selling games and valuations]]

- Real investors want developers with past popular, well-monetized games. You must be the owner or main creator.
- Most want a playable game with a monetization plan. They check CCU holding, retention and ARPPU (average revenue per paying user).
- Run test ads first and show D1 (day-1 retention) after a few days.
- Pre-revenue investment is risky, and very high pre-revenue valuations are essentially unheard of.
- Nobody funds full ads for 10% of an unreleased game from a developer with no prior hits.
- Find investors through connections, Twitter/X and YouTube content. Friends with a few hundred thousand Robux can fund ads instead.
- Most self-described investors are not genuine. Real ones pay first; one wired funds within 3 hours.
- An investor who thinks ads are expensive is a red flag.
- Keep funds for at least 7–14 days of ads, so pseudo-investors cannot take 51% just for running ads.
- Give investors Creator Hub ad permissions, not Studio or Team Create access.
- Price equity as investment divided by valuation. A $30 investment in a $1k game is 3%.
- For ad-only money, prefer a revenue share until payback plus profit, not permanent equity.
- Give investors no more than about 25% of a game.
- Many investors contribute little beyond thumbnails; some just throw money and leave.
- A good investor acts like a consultant or PM. The server owner takes percentages for PM and design work, not cash.
- If a game is already crushing, you probably do not need an investor.
- Renegotiate an investor's share only if their required funding shrinks. Otherwise reduce your own share.
- Minors can have a guardian or adult co-signer sign. One teen lost a $2.5k offer when parents refused.
- Live ops is a weak pitch alone. Bundle it with ad spend or asset work.
- Never trust a studio's live-ops promise without a track record. Revenue-share groups taking ~20% have presided over collapses.
- Live-ops studios rarely take over a game for less than about 50%.
- Big studios buy around 10k CCU, run about $250 a day of ads per game, retune monetization and staff live ops.
- One such game went from about 10k to 200k CCU after purchase.
- Some investors only back games they expect to reach 75–100k CCU.
- SimpleBricks Studio invests in or publishes games, taking about 50% for marketing.

### Typical investment and equity terms

| Deal type | Typical terms | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Small ad funding for equity | $150–500 of ads for 15–25% | High |
| Daily ad credits for equity | 16–40 credits a day for 10–14 days for 10% | High |
| Larger ad credit packages | 150–420 credits for 15–18% | Medium |
| Cash for equity, small game | $2k–5k for 30–35% at 40–700 CCU | High |
| Robux funding | 100k–200k Robux for 20–45%; 35k–40k for 4–10% | Medium |
| Full funding, ads, management and analytics | 40–50% | Medium |
| Revenue share until payback | 20% until 2.5x returned; 50% until the sum is repaid | Medium |
| Predatory offers | 50–95% for 1k–10k Robux or $20–40 of ads | High |
