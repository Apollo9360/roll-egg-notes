# Selling games and valuations

Up: [[Marketing, Teams and Business]] · Previous: [[Investors and publishers]] · Next: [[Scams and safe deals]]

- Do not sell before reaching 250 HEP and running ads. Many games sold early later grew 10–100x.
    - Examples: 300 CCU sold for $2k reached 26k; 50 CCU sold for $1.2k reached 1.2k in three days.
- Never sell full ownership of a growing game. Sell a percentage, unless the buyer is a trusted, real studio.
- Sell only if you doubt the game long-term, are burnt out, or after about 6 months of evaluation.
- Studios pay well but often abandon games. Sell cash-grabs; keep a say in passion projects.
- Outcomes after sale vary: one studio grew a game from 1k to 3k CCU; others let games die or got them banned.
- Value depends on revenue and trajectory. Compare similar games; Rocquire, a game marketplace, is a useful reference.
- Validate games with your own research or RoTrends and RoAnalytics before investing.
- Buyers make the first offer. Sale price depends more on stats and potential than on CCU.
- Studios will buy at about 50 CCU with strong stats, such as 90th-percentile playtime, D1 and D7 (day-7 retention), and 99th-percentile PTR (play-through rate).
- Low-ARPDAU (average revenue per daily active user) genres get lowballed even at 10k CCU.
- Do not buy old games averaging about 5 CCU.
- Ownership transfers move the group or the game. See Roblox's [game ownership transfer docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/projects/game-ownership-transfer).
