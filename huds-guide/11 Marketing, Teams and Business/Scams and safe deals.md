# Scams and safe deals

Up: [[Marketing, Teams and Business]] · Previous: [[Selling games and valuations]] · Next: [[Estimates and projections (Business)]]

- Red flags:
    - Wanting ownership before a contract or payment.
    - Pressure to join a group, or a price quoted without analytics.
    - Unverifiable agency claims and few mutual contacts.
    - The word "passion" in a commission post, or percent-only offers.
    - Threats to copy the game unless you sell it all.
- Verify scouts via a developer rank in the claimed game's group, and verify embedded links.
- Reverse-image search studio portfolios. Copy-paste partnership messages are usually scams.
- Server roles are not proof of trust. People have prepaid $170–200 to fake moderators.
- Fake studios have used Zoom calls with a "lawyer" to slip in remote-access requests.
- Fake contracts, websites and gamepass purchase videos have been used to take games up to 40k CCU.
- Some buyers delete a game and re-upload it under another group.
- Contractors who take 50% upfront sometimes vanish with sums from 22k to 125k Robux.

Safe sale procedure:

1. Sign a legal contract with IP terms. Use a lawyer for big deals and watch for vague clawback clauses.
2. Settle ownership terms before any money moves.
3. Get the money before transferring ownership, because contracts are hard to enforce.
4. If using escrow, use a verified third party, never one the buyer provides.
5. Escrow flow: the buyer pays escrow, you hand over the game, then escrow pays you.
6. Accept that no transfer is 100% safe.

Common losses:

- Large sales are sometimes never paid, as with a 9k-CCU game at about 225k.
- Fake escrow links are common. A 3k-CCU game earning about $100k a month went for $10k for 80% this way.
- Cross-border DMCA claims lapse unless you file a lawsuit within about 10 days of a counter-notice.
- Roblox Support generally will not return a transferred game.
