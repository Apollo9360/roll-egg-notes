# Off-platform marketing versus ads

Up: [[Marketing, Teams and Business]] · Next: [[What social and creator content achieves]]

- Most experienced developers say organic social media rarely works alone, and growing without ads is rare.
- With no ad budget, the realistic routes are short-form video plus luck, searchable trends, or an existing community.
- With $0 for ads, learn to make good short-form content (owner-recommended).
- Marketing amplifies a game whose stats are already good. A social audience will not stay if retention and playtime are poor.
- Social suits games with a tight-knit community, a niche or a clippable concept. For broad-appeal games, ads are easier.
- For +1 and incremental games, TikTok and YouTube are generally not worth it; Roblox ads work better.
- Ads versus socials is disputed:
    - The majority view: ads are faster, and the ad manager is sufficient for nearly every game.
    - A minority says organic players show much better stats than ad players.
    - When ads fail, the usual causes are bad retention, no reason to stay 10+ minutes, no reason to return and no reason to buy.
- A common combined plan: ads first, then TikTok, Shorts and creator videos to keep players once ads have done their job.
- A dedicated community is the easiest way to reach the 250 HEP (highly engaged players) all-ages threshold without ads.
    - The alternative is the 50,000 Robux expedited fee.
    - The threshold was 500 at launch in late May and 250 from about Aug 19. A drop to 100 is announced for November.
- Social alone generally reaches 250 HEP only with about 10k subscribers or 500 loyal fans.
- Games targeted at 16+ audiences can be marketed through social media without ads (low confidence).
    - A game made 16+ by Roblox later lost ground despite viral TikToks.
- Other non-ad channels: Instagram, Twitter/X, a Discord server, trendy titles, creator videos and word of mouth.
- TikTok pays nothing to developers; posting about your game is purely advertising.
