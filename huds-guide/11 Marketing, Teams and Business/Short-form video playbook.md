# Short-form video playbook

Up: [[Marketing, Teams and Business]] · Previous: [[What social and creator content achieves]] · Next: [[Paying creators and clippers]]

1. Start posting during development. Accounts started after launch often get single-digit views for weeks.
2. Search TikTok for "in this roblox game" and copy proven formats with a twist (owner-recommended).
3. Hook immediately. The first 3 seconds are critical, so get into the action fast.
4. Record at higher resolution and hide the HUD (heads-up display). Heavy UI (user interface) fails in vertical format.
5. Keep editing snappy and satisfying. Use an AI voice or your own voiceover with pop-in subtitles.
6. End with a call to action, such as joining your community.
7. Post consistently, often 3–4 times a day, and expect most posts to flop.

Tactics and observations:

- Short-form is consistency and luck. One video got 300 views, then 4M on re-upload.
- Some developers run several accounts before a video hits (low confidence on safe account limits).
- Clip-ability matters. A distinctive, clippable concept gets searched; a game identical to others will not.
- Study similar Shorts with 100k+ views and copy what works.
- Post the same clips to TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and Facebook. Reposting TikToks as Shorts underperformed for some (disputed).
- Platform audiences differ: TikTok is youngest, YouTube is middle, and Reels skew oldest.
- Brainrot and slop-genre promos often attract dislikes and hate comments on TikTok (disputed). Some suggest Shorts for brainrot content.
- Devlog videos get views (about 100–250k is common) but conversion to players is disputed. One vehicle game marketed entirely through devlogs.
- A plain game screenshot outperformed edited videos once (low confidence).
- Add small deliberate mistakes, like misspellings, to bait comments.
- For party and horror games, clip funny, exciting or trolly moments with friends.
- A suggested trailer structure: noob-to-pro, then an RNG (random number generator) scene, then the best VFX (visual effects), then a challenge line.
- Add a video to the game page as higher-quality onboarding.
- A fresh YouTube channel is hard without about 50k subscribers. Use TikTok, X and Instagram instead.
- Search YouTube for videos of people playing your game. Players narrate their thoughts, which is great feedback.
- Roblox Moments is barely used, and several reports say it is full of inappropriate content.
