# Show the core fantasy and loop

Up: [[Thumbnails and Icons]] · Previous: [[CTR targets and economics]] · Next: [[Composition, contrast and text]]

- The thumbnail and title together sell an appealing fantasy: the core idea, such as "hacking a business."
- The thumbnail highlights moments of that fantasy so players click to experience it.
- The thumbnail must explain the game in one image. Kids need to see exactly what they would be playing.
- It should read within about 100 pixels.
- Ask why a player would click it. A thumbnail that does not explain the game also hurts new-player playtime.
- The player sees about 10 other games at once. Design to win that competition.
- Show the action, not the object: the stone mid-skip, not just held.
- Focus on the core loop, not a side or flex mechanic. Show the table game, not the chairs.
- A "Dig for Treasure" game should show digging up tempting treasure, not pets or shovel upgrades.
- Show the hook. A troll tower must show the troll part.
- Make the key item special. If everything is gold, make the stolen item diamond.
- Sell the power fantasy and oversell it. A level-999 vault should look huge and full of money.
- Show the player strong and winning, never weak or merely watching. Progression thumbnails go weak but winning, then stronger, then overpowered.
- Theme matters. Stealing is more appealing than defending.
- Generic thumbnails get low CTR. Zombie PvE (player versus environment) is among the most saturated themes; show your unique point.
- All thumbnails in a set must depict the same game, in one consistent style.
- Thumbnails need not be literal in-game shots, as long as the fantasy is true.
- Compare against similar games already pulling players and ask what theirs has that yours lacks.
- Ask people who play the genre, ideally 10–20, which thumbnails they would click.
- A thumbnail can work in one genre and fail completely in another.
- Plan packaging during design: ask who would play the game and how it will look as a thumbnail.
- Thumbnails get clicks, not concurrent users (CCU). A good thumbnail cannot save a game with a bad first session.
