# Estimates and projections (Thumbnails)

Up: [[Thumbnails and Icons]] · Previous: [[AI workflows, tools and artists]] · Next: [[Examples of winners and flops]]

All figures in this section are estimates derived from reported data, not reported facts.

| Estimate | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| CTR noise at 1k impressions | About ±1.1 points at 3% CTR | 95% binomial interval at p = 0.03 |
| CTR noise at 10k impressions | About ±0.33 points at 3% CTR | Same formula; matches the common 10k judging point |
| CTR noise at 100k impressions | About ±0.1 points | Same formula; explains why CTR rarely moves after 100k |
| Impressions to tell 3% from 4% | About 5–6k per thumbnail | Two-proportion test, 95% confidence, 80% power |
| Impressions to tell 3% from 3.5% | About 20k per thumbnail | Same test; matches the 20–25k reliable threshold |
| Cost to test 25 thumbnails to 10k each | About $25–250 | 250k impressions at a reported $0.1–1 per 1k impressions |
| Thumbnails needed for one 5%+ winner | About 14–22 for 90% odds | 10–15% hit rate from 2–5 winners per 25 |
| Plays per $100 of ads | About 11k–50k | Reported CPP of 0.002–0.009 |
| Plays from 100k impressions at 3% CTR | About 2,000–2,500 | Interpolated between the 1.5% and 4.5% rows, 70–80% click-to-play |
| AI vs commissioned cost for 25 thumbnails | About $1 vs $175–5,000 | 1–5 cents vs $7–200 per image |

- Estimate: raising CTR from 3% to 5% cuts cost per play by about 40% (basis: CPP scales inversely with CTR).
- Estimate: at the expected 100 HEP threshold, the same CTR gets there about 2.5x faster than at 250 (basis: impressions scale with HEP count).
- Estimate: a 1-point QPTR gain on 100k daily home impressions adds about 1,000 plays a day (basis: QPTR is plays per impression).
- Rule of thumb: never judge a thumbnail that has fewer than about 1,000 impressions or under 24 hours of ads.
