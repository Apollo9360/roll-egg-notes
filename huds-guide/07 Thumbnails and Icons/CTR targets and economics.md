# CTR targets and economics

Up: [[Thumbnails and Icons]] · Next: [[Show the core fantasy and loop]]

Click-through rate (CTR) is clicks divided by impressions. Targets vary by genre and drifted upward over 2026.

| CTR | Typical reading | Notes | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 1% | Players actively avoid the game | Rare; delete a 0.5% creative | High |
| 1–2% | Weak | Games under 2% get almost nothing from the ad system | High |
| 2–3% | Borderline | First-ever campaigns often land here; about 3% is fair for RNG (random number generator) and steal games | Medium |
| 3% | Majority floor | Most scrap anything below 3% (owner-recommended floor) | High |
| 4–5% | Good | The owner's ideal target; 4%+ widely called good | High |
| 5–8% | Very good | 5%+ is the common ideal; 7–8% is the practical ceiling on large samples | High |
| 8–10% | Excellent | Roughly top-tier; usually needs a strong title, idea or trend | Medium |
| 10–16% | Exceptional | Reported on hundreds of thousands of impressions, mostly trend or meme thumbnails | Medium |
| 20%+ | Outlier | Usually a small sample that regresses; claims are often doubted | Low |

- Most experienced developers treat 3% as the floor and 4% as the goal.
- Some set stricter bars: 4%+ for every genre, or 5%+ before spending more credits (disputed).
- A minority keep anything above 2–2.2% unless other creatives are at 3.5–4%.
- For fast progress toward the highly engaged player (HEP) threshold, 4.5–6%+ is suggested.
- Situational targets:
    - Multiplayer games need above 5%.
    - Games restricted to 16+ can realistically expect 3–5%.
    - If a bad impression ratio cannot be fixed, aim for 8%+ to keep cost per play (CPP) low.
- The gap between 3–4% and 8%+ comes mostly from the title, game idea and trend demand, not the art.
- A good thumbnail can lift CTR by roughly 50–80%. Do not expect it to turn 1% into 3% on a weak concept.
- Trend-based games get high clicks from sheer demand (owner-recommended observation).

### CTR by genre

| Genre | Typical CTR | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Tower and trend games | 7–10% easily, though players may be low value | Medium |
| Brainrot and meme games | 7–10%+ with absurd, cash-grab styles | Medium |
| Incrementals and basic tycoons | Rarely above 4–5%; often 1–3% | Medium |
| Sandbox automation tycoons | About 1.2% without brainrot-style art; one reached 8% on 12M impressions | Low |
| Anime games | Driven mostly by the source anime and game name | Low |

### Why CTR drives cost

On plays-objective ads you mostly pay for impressions, so CTR sets the cost per play.

| Impressions | CTR | Clicks | Approximate plays |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100k | 1.5% | 1,500 | 1,000–1,200 |
| 100k | 4.5% | 4,500 | About 3,700 |

- Tripling CTR makes each play about 3x cheaper.
- Reported good results: about 0.002–0.009 CPP at 3–8.5% CTR. Aim for CPP under 0.005 on low spend.
- Engagement-objective ads show higher CTR (about 2–3%) than Plays ads (about 1.5%), but Plays ads bring more players.
- A short, 6% CTR burst was credited with making an ad about 5x more effective (low confidence).
