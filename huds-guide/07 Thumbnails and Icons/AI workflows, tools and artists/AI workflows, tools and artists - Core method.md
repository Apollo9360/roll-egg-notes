# AI workflows, tools and artists - Core method

Up: [[AI workflows, tools and artists]] · Next: [[AI workflows, tools and artists - Named workflows]]

- The owner's advice is to make thumbnails yourself with AI; he also hires GFX artists who use AI.
- Do not just prompt. Feed references: in-game screenshots, your assets, and winning thumbnails from your genre.
- One reference plus a short, clear prompt beats elaborate prompts alone.
- Explain the whole game first, then give a reference, then ask.
- Keep 100% of your own assets and borrow only composition, lighting, angle and readability from references.
- Prompt checklist: 16:9, one strong focal point, bold contrast, black outlines, readable silhouettes, mobile readability, no logos, UI or clutter.
- Generate without text, and add text and faces yourself, since AI handles both poorly.
- Generate a sheet of 4 thumbnails in one image, then crop the best.
- Watch for artifacts: extra legs, bad hands, muddy clothing, uncanny grass. Ask for a drawn style and avoid the glossy AI look.
- Making thumbnails "less AI" and more interesting lifted one game from 1.9% to 6.2% CTR.
- Good results take iteration, not one-shot prompts. Quality can degrade after many edits in one chat.
- Do not reuse the exact prompt across tools, or you get the same image.
- Upscale noisy AI output with ChatGPT and remove model watermarks.
