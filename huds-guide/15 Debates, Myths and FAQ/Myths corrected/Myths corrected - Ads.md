# Myths corrected - Ads

Up: [[Myths corrected]] · Previous: [[All-ages access and HEP]] · Next: [[Myths corrected - Thumbnails and AI]]

| Myth | Reality | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Ads pay for themselves | They rarely pay back. Ads buy HEP and data; home recommendations make the money. | High |
| More ad spend buys growth or home recommendations | Spend buys data faster, not better stats. In one owner test, $200/day for a week added only about 300 CCU. | High |
| A huge budget on an untested game is safe | It can show the algorithm that players dislike the game. Polish first. | Medium |
| Two weeks of ads guarantees the algorithm | Many games never get recommendations. Stats decide. | High |
| You need a month of ads because of day-28 retention | Many games reached recommendations within 3–9 days. Early day-1 behavior drives the prediction. | High |
| Fast-growing games spend $100/day on ads | They are usually established studios, exceptional games or viral marketing. | Medium |
| Ad or search plays build QPTR | Only home-recommendation impressions count toward qualified play-through rate (QPTR). | High |
| High click-through rate (CTR) means a good ad | Low cost per play (CPP) and downstream stats matter most. High CTR can bring cold, low-intent traffic. | High |
| Better thumbnails earn more ad impressions | Spend sets impressions. Creatives only shift allocation, and Plays ads optimize for plays. | Medium |
| 2% ad CTR is good | Most experienced developers set 3% as the minimum. | High |
| CTR under 3% means the game is dead | CTR is a thumbnail problem, separate from retention. | Medium |
| Sub-1% CTR is normal for two-player games | It signals a thumbnail or title problem; two-player games reach 2%+. | Low |
| High CTR on a tiny sample beats 3% at scale | Small samples swing wildly; one fell from 17% to 2%. A 1% CTR at 1k impressions won't become 5% either. | High |
| CTR never changes during a campaign | It can rise or fall as learning settles. | Medium |
| The learning phase takes 1–2 hours | It takes at least about 24 hours. CTR often drops or stays flat afterwards. | High |
| Pausing ads pauses learning | Turning a campaign off and on resets learning. | High |
| You can't add creatives to a running campaign | Use the three dots, then Edit; the campaign returns to learning. Published creatives can only be disabled; the maximum is 25. | Medium |
| A new campaign inherits old learning | It starts fresh. Duplicated campaigns showed very different CTRs. | Medium |
| 16 credits/day is per thumbnail | It is the campaign total. | Medium |
| Cancelling a campaign wastes money | Unspent credits stay in the balance. | High |
| Ad players are bots | Plays ads bring real but untargeted players. A few idle accounts early in learning are reported. | Medium |
| Ad stats equal home-rec stats | Ad players show shorter playtime, lower spend and often 2–4x lower day-1 retention. Stats usually rebound once recommendations dominate. | High |
| Ad stats don't matter | Roblox documentation says ad signals can speed recommendation consideration. Terrible ad stats predict poor algorithm results. | Medium |
| Ad CCU shows how good a game is | Ad CCU is temporary; games peaking at 400 CCU on ads have flopped. Judge stats. | High |
| Players from a big ad push will stay | Most leave when ads stop. Spread spend instead. | Medium |
| A 300-CCU spike on day 3–4 means the algorithm hit | Usually an ad-spend burst. Check for home-rec impressions. | High |
| Ads cause CCU to drop | Ads don't remove players; ad traffic arrives in spikes. | Medium |
| Time of day or weekday matters for ads | Mostly no. Ads run all day across time zones. | Medium |
| Narrow targeting gets better players | It raises CTR but worsens audience fit and shrinks reach. Go broad (owner-recommended). | High |
| Including ages 13–17 wastes money | Roblox only serves ads to users who can play the game. | Low |
| Roblox rejects AI thumbnails in ads | Rejections have other causes. | Low |
| Buying Robux for ads saves money | Card credits are $1 each. Via Robux a credit costs about 236–285 Robux, near its Developer Exchange (DevEx) value, so bought Robux cost about 2.6x more. | High |
| Earned Robux are a bad way to pay | At DevEx value, 16 credits cost about 4,200–4,560 Robux, roughly $16–17. | Medium |
| CPM is impressions divided by spend | Cost per mille (CPM) is spend per 1,000 impressions. | High |
| A 16+ rating hurts CTR | CTR depends on the thumbnail. | Low |
