# Typical costs and timelines

Up: [[Age Gates and Highly Engaged Players]] · Previous: [[Reaching the threshold with ads]] · Next: [[Tracking progress and review time]]

| Situation | Typical outcome | Range seen | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 500 era, ~16 credits/day, good stats | 500 HEP in 3–7 days | ~$70–200 total; owner estimate ~$100 | High |
| 500 era, weak game or low budget | Stalls short of 500 | 45–220 HEP after 2–10 days; $370 for 70 | High |
| 250 era, 15–30 credits/day Plays, good stats | 250 HEP in 2–5 days | ~30–100 credits | High |
| 250 era, Plays plus Engagement | 250 in 2–4 days | e.g. 16 + 8 or 10 + 6 per day | Medium |
| 250 era, Engagement heavy | 75–250 HEP in 2–5 days | $12 gave 110; $30 gave 114; $100 gave 170 | Medium |
| 250 era, weak CTR or saturated genre | Under 100 HEP after $40–80 | 3–35 HEP from $16–50 | High |
| Good playtime but few spenders | Slow even with good stats | 242 HEP after 10+ days at 16/day | Medium |
| Never qualifies | Stop and rework | 150–450 credits without reaching 250 | Medium |

- About $0.2–1 per HEP is typical on good runs. Poor runs exceed $4 per HEP.
- Common totals for 250: "about 50–100 ad credits", "$50 minimum", "$30 if the game is good".
- Saturated genres take longer. A novel concept can get there cheaply despite weak execution.

### When to give up or stop ads

- Signals of a winner: home recs within half a week. No home recs after 2 weeks means a flop.
- Stop spending if all ages is not reached within about 6–10 days of ads.
- If playtime, D1 or D7 sit below about the 20th percentile after ads, do not continue.
- Do not cut ads at the threshold unless home recs are present. Reaching 250 without home recs usually means the game dies.
- Other stop signals: ~30k–100k home-rec impressions per day, or home recs alone holding 100–150 CCU.
