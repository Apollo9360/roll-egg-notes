# Pay the fee or run ads

Up: [[Age Gates and Highly Engaged Players]] · Previous: [[Fees publishing fee, Plus and the expedite fee]] · Next: [[Reaching the threshold with ads]]

| Factor | Pay 50,000 Robux | Run ads to 250 |
|---|---|---|
| Cash outlay | 50,000 Robux locked ~90 days, then refundable | ~$30–100 typical, not refunded |
| Time to all ages | Hours to ~2 days | ~3–7 days plus 1–3 days review |
| Still need ads | Yes, for data and the algorithm | Ads are the route itself |
| Risk | Forfeit on removal or failed conditions | Money burned if the game never qualifies |
| Best for | Trend games, kid-targeted games, idle earned Robux | Most games, first games, tight budgets |
| Confidence | High | High |

- The owner pays the fee before running ads, calling it worth it for speed, especially for trend games (owner-recommended).
- In one owner comparison, a paid game was approved in ~48 hours versus 11 days organically.
- The owner's ordering: pay the fee, then run Plays ads, not Engagement ads.
- The owner also advises going all in only when confident; spend less when unsure.
- Most other developers say ads reach 250 for far less and give you data while doing it.
- Common view: if a game cannot reach 250 with 16+ players, it would likely flop at all ages too.
- Paying makes sense with idle earned Robux, a short-lived trend, or a game only kids will enjoy.
- Some run 7–10 days of 16+ ads first, then pay only once analytics look promising.
- Another pattern: earn Robux with a 16+ game, then use it to expedite a kids' game.
- One developer regretted spending 500,000 Robux across five games, since ads unlocked all ages by day 3 anyway.
- Games have reached 2k+ CCU without paying.
- With the 100-HEP threshold coming in November, ads become even cheaper than the fee.
