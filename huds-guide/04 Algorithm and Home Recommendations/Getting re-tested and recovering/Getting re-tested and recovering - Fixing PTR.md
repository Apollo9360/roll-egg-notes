# Getting re-tested and recovering - Fixing PTR

Up: [[Getting re-tested and recovering]] · Next: [[Getting re-tested and recovering - What kills recs]]

- If stats are good but PTR is bad, fix thumbnails and the name. Ignoring PTR is a common reason good games never blow up.
- Low PTR reflects a weak idea; weak other metrics reflect execution, which is easier to iterate.
- A 4th-percentile PTR can drag down a game with every other stat at the 85th percentile or higher.
- PTR causes: thumbnails, the name, the first 60 seconds, how thumbnails hold up at scale, and demand for the fantasy.
- Doubling PTR roughly doubles players. Small PTR gains move home impressions a lot.
- At 800 CCU, 1.2% PTR loses many players compared with 1.5–2%.
- At scale, a 0.1-point swing in PTR means tens of thousands of players.
- Updating weak home thumbnails is the biggest lever. Place → Thumbnails shows home-rec stats per thumbnail.
- Upload dedicated home-recommendation thumbnails; many beginners forget and get no home recs (owner-recommended).
- Some report Roblox falls back to detail-page thumbnails when none are uploaded (low confidence).
- Run 3–5 home-rec thumbnails when recs first start, not one.
- A thumbnail video reportedly lifts PTR about 30% (low confidence).
- A better title can lift CTR, for example from 2.5% to above 3%, or triple QPTR.
- With tiny qualified-play counts, PTR swings wildly. Wait at least one more day before replacing thumbnails.
- Thumbnail testing is still worth it under personalization. Keep testing many thumbnails.
- Keep ad CTR high, so PTR is not poor when home recs begin.
