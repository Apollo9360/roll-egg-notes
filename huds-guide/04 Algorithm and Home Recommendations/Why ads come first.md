# Why ads come first

Up: [[Algorithm and Home Recommendations]] · Previous: [[What the algorithm ranks on]] · Next: [[When home recs start]]

- Ads generate player data so Roblox can decide whether to push the game. Their main job is to establish the retention stat.
- Ads also tell the algorithm which kind of home-rec players to suggest the game to.
- Once in home recs, the game is judged mainly on home-rec player stats.
- Roblox docs say ads, search, charts, friends and notifications can all accelerate consideration. Ad stats therefore count somewhat.
- If ad stats sit near the 10th percentile, home-rec players rarely lift them; even 300 credits/day will not fix it.
- Run ads consistently for at least a week, or the algorithm will not have enough data.
- About 1k visits a day gives reasonably accurate stats.
- Turning ads off early resets learning (owner-recommended: keep them on until home recs take over).
- Before home recs, CCU roughly equals ad spend × CTR (click-through rate) × detail-page CTR.
- Sitting near 20 CCU on ads alone is normal. CCU during ads does not matter; stats do.
- Traction without ads is rare. Games without ads often see under 100 impressions a week.
- Roblox does not carry learned audiences across campaigns. Run longer single campaigns rather than many short ones.
- Small ad tests earn a low home-rec rating. About 18 credits over a week reaches very few people.
- Leave ad targeting unset and let Roblox optimize.

### How much to spend

- The common budget is 12–16 credits/day; 5–10 credits/day has also reached home recs, more slowly.
- Spending more mainly gets a game into home recs faster. It does not make a weak game succeed.
- If confident in the stats, about 32 credits/day can bring home recs within a week.
- Go all in only when you expect the game to do well; otherwise spend less (owner-recommended).
- Big spend on a weak game can depress stats with low-intent traffic.
- Good early stats mean less ad spend and an earlier, stronger push.
- If 2–3 weeks of ads still show bad stats, iterate on the game instead of spending more.
- If playtime, D1 and D7 sit under the 20th percentile on ads, quit or rework. Aim for the 90th percentile.

### Home-rec players vs ad players

- Home-rec players are higher quality and more on-target. Stats often double once home recs take over.
- Typical playtime gaps: 10–12 minutes on ads versus 15–35 minutes from home recs.
- Home-rec players count as highly engaged far more often than ad players.
- About 10k home impressions are worth roughly 50k ad impressions.
- Ad players are often the stats bottleneck. Pausing ads for 2 days while shipping an update has improved stats immediately.
- Exceptions exist: some +1 evolution games show identical stats from both sources, and some home-rec waves did worse than ads.
- If ad players beat home-rec players, the ad audience may be new to the genre.
- If home-rec players underperform, the game may look too similar to competitors.
- Ad CTR does not transfer to home recs. A high-CTR ad thumbnail can perform poorly in personalized recs.
