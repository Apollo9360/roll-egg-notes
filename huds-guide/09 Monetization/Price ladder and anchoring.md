# Price ladder and anchoring

Up: [[Monetization]] · Previous: [[Product types]] · Next: [[Cheap entry items and starter packs]]

| Tier | Example prices | Purpose | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | 1–49 Robux | First purchase, PCR | High |
| Core | About 99–300 Robux | 2x passes, packs, VIP | High |
| High | 499–8,000 Robux | Whales, bundles, characters | High |
| Top | 9,999–15,999 Robux | A few whale items or top multipliers | Medium |

- Shared ladders: 9/19/39/199/399; or a cheap 9-Robux item, useful mid passes, and about 199 for main-advantage passes.
- A full shop should total at least about 3,000 Robux if a player buys everything.
- Multiplier ladder from an experienced developer: x2 3, x5 27, x10 67, x20 154, x40 356, x100 817, x300 1,999, up to x10,000 at 15,999 Robux.
- Typical guidance for 2x cash: about 300–500 Robux as a gamepass, 49–99 as a developer product. Newer games often sell 2x for 3–10 Robux (disputed).
- New games usually price core passes around 100 Robux. Avoid 900-Robux passes on a first game.
- Core items above about 300 Robux (e.g. VIP at 575) are usually too high early. Pull them down (399 to 299); raise later.
- Many keep items at or under about 200 Robux unless the item is very powerful (disputed).
- Bundle gamepasses 20–30% cheaper than buying them individually.
- Compare prices to similar games. Overpriced passes in a simple game hurt sales.
- Too-cheap core items also lose money; 2x money and offline earnings are often underpriced.
- When the cheapest passes sell best, novelty or troll passes (e.g. "jumpscare all") can be priced higher.
- Scale cash packs with progress (rebirths or a percentage of balance). Fixed small packs become worthless as players get richer.
- Price time skips by time saved. If a player earns 25k cash per hour and needs 100k, 100k cash for about 79 Robux fits.

### Anchoring and charm pricing

- Show a higher price crossed out, e.g. 999 struck through, now 450. The red "sale" price needs a visible strike-through line.
- Only anchor to a real price. Run the higher price for a day or two first, or use a separate product at the higher price.
- Showing a sale price on the HUD that differs from the shop price risks false-advertising rules (low confidence).
- Showing the real total value (e.g. about 700 Robux worth) beats "98% off".
- Charm pricing: use 99 instead of 100 and 1,799 instead of 2,000.
- A one-time 50% discount on 2x or large cash products creates FOMO (fear of missing out).

### Testing a price change

1. Wait for enough traffic. 10–20 CCU is too noisy for A/B tests.
2. Change prices and run for a couple of days.
3. Track income per product, not just total.
4. Revert if revenue drops.

- "Door slam" test: raise prices about 40%. If sales drop, settle at +20%.
- Increases of 20–50% are common; raise top sellers 20–30% and double clearly underpriced items.
- Raising prices often barely hurts conversion; kids may prefer pricier products.
- Start low, then raise prices around 500 CCU. Raise any very cheap product that sells heavily.
- Entry items tolerate big relative increases; 3 to 9 Robux on a 2x-speed pass lost only a couple of sales.
