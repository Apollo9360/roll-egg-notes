# Cheap entry items and starter packs

Up: [[Monetization]] · Previous: [[Price ladder and anchoring]] · Next: [[High-end items, bundles and upsells]]

- Cheap products carry PCR. Ultra-cheap microtransactions explain most very high PCR.
- Results: a 1-Robux first spin or a 3-Robux starter pack reached 90th+ percentile PCR with ARPPU near 1–2 Robux.
- A 29-Robux starter pack gets about 3% PCR. A 9-Robux item that about 70% of players buy also lifts sales of 499-Robux packs.
- A cheapest gamepass of 59 Robux hurts PCR.
- The first purchase is the hardest; buyers convert more easily and play longer afterward.
- Kids barely notice the difference between 2–3 and 19–29 Robux. Many hold 3–10 leftover Robux that 1-Robux items capture.
- Offer entry products at the moment of need, e.g. a 3-Robux (not 9) skip-spin while players wait.
- Typical entry products: 2x power for 3 Robux, 2x money at about 9 Robux, 1.5x for 1 Robux, 2x speed for 3 Robux in +1 games.
- 9-Robux "skip timer", "kill all" or "nuke all" products drive very high PCR. Purchase announcements in chat add social proof.
- Escalating developer products beat one 2x pass, e.g. x2 → x4 → x8 at 9 → 18 Robux and rising.
- A 9-Robux pack boosts conversion but makes 100+ Robux passes feel expensive. About 19 Robux is a common compromise.
- Starter packs exist only for PCR and should not be your best seller. Repeat purchases need deeper offers.
- A 2x offline-earnings developer product typically sells at 29–39 Robux. Cheapest passes are often around 49 Robux.
- Do not give too much value cheaply; a 3-Robux product granting 1B power broke progression.
- A small product that does not convert needs more value, e.g. "fill 25 keycaps" instead of a smaller amount.
- Short free boosts (2x luck for 10 minutes or less) do not hurt sales if given sparingly, and can help.
- If 2x breaks your economy, sell +10% (+0.1x) money instead.
- Cheap items carry costs (disputed):
    - Very low prices (1–4 Robux) can tank ARPPU and the in-game economy.
    - Some feel 3-Robux products make a game feel like shovelware and hurt long-term retention.
    - Big games often start at about 299 instead of 3.
    - Cheap products help conversion, not retention.
