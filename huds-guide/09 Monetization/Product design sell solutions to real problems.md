# Product design sell solutions to real problems

Up: [[Monetization]] · Previous: [[Placement in-world, menus and popups]] · Next: [[Regional and managed pricing]]

- Monetization comes last in development, but think about audience first because it decides spending (owner-recommended).
- Fun comes first; a fun game can be monetized later (owner-recommended).
- FOMO, scarcity and urgency together reliably sell, at every age (owner-recommended).
- Find friction in a repetitive, rewarding loop and sell relief. If everyone progresses easily with 0 Robux, no one buys.
- Fair-play rule: keep the core loop playable free, and let payment reduce a natural pain. Do not invent pains.
- Turn friction messages into products, e.g. replace "Cannot change combat speed" with a 2x-speed prompt.
- If AFK players get robbed, sell a 24-hour base lock. Do not also sell a force-open product unless its price scales with the lock.
- Place gamepasses at economy bottlenecks after doing the math.
- Robux options on abilities work because younger players get frustrated and pay to skip.
- A guaranteed restock product beats a random 5-Robux restock. Capping stock at 1 forces a choice between paying and waiting about 5 minutes.
- Roll-to-buy: when a roll lands an item the player cannot afford, offer it for Robux. Copying this nearly tripled one game's revenue (owner-recommended).
- Price rolls by tier, e.g. one Robux price for a mythic roll and a lower one for a rare roll (owner-recommended).
- Timed locks: a box "unlocking in 14 minutes" with a skip product raised PCR and playtime by about 10 percentiles.
- Queue or waiting games sell "skip the line" products well, even with weak retention.
- Split one large "skip all" pass into per-instance developer products for better conversion.
- Time skips sell: 15 Robux for 5 minutes, about 79 Robux to skip an hour, or about 100 Robux for 7 days of cash.
- Boost durations vary widely, from 3 minutes to 24 hours. About 20 Robux per 15 minutes is a common anchor.
- Paid spins beat "unlimited free spins, then pay for the item", which feels like bait-and-switch.
- Sell gameplay add-ons (e.g. a knockback shove) for Robux directly, not coins (owner-recommended).
- Monetize expression: parts and blocks from crates can replace skins in building games (owner-recommended).
- Monetization is a byproduct of good progression; engaged players buy 2x to finish faster.
- Trading and trade tokens can lift revenue, and update days bring 2–3x revenue.
- Seasonal events (Halloween, Christmas) with new items sell well. Event currency for skins extends lifespan.
- Daily rewards: make Day 2 and Day 7 stand out. Add a "Skip all" Robux option for long sequences.
- Rebirths: give a flat 2x income per rebirth. Do not stack rebirth, prestige and ascension within a few clicks.
- Casual games monetize poorly because there is no felt challenge. Add conversion points later without ruining early play.
- In PvE, pay-to-win is fine unless one buyer ruins it for free players; avoid "nuke the server" passes.
- A round-based game with no lobby has nowhere to monetize; add one.
