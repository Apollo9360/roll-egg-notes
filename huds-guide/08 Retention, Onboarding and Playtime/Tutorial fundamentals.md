# Tutorial fundamentals

Up: [[Retention, Onboarding and Playtime]] · Previous: [[Benchmarks and priorities]] · Next: [[Visual guidance and tutorial UI]]

- Get players to the fun fast; top games give instant comprehension of where to go and how to progress within 30–60 seconds (owner-recommended).
- Players should understand the game within about 10 seconds; assume 6–10-year-olds who leave without a reward in about 8 seconds.
- The tutorial should teach the core loop only; start it at the game's core moment.
- Core loop means action, challenge, reward, such as roll a stat, fight with it, earn currency, upgrade to roll better.
- If the game cannot be explained in a few words, kids will not bother.
- A child or a non-English speaker should understand the game from what is on screen alone.
- Cut steps not vital to the core loop, such as feedback or leaderboard explanations; show the core loop before purchasing chores.
- Fulfil the title's promise at once: a "be a ___" game should spawn players as that thing immediately.
- A game with essentially no tutorial usually has a big problem (owner-recommended).
- Add a small, easy objective between the tutorial and the main goal, such as "upgrade your item 5 times".
- Onboarding should also present progression: why the player upgrades and grinds, not just how.
- Some known-genre clones and 16+ games succeed with little hand-holding (low confidence).
- FPS (first-person shooter) games can replace the tutorial with quests or a practice range, as Rivals does.
