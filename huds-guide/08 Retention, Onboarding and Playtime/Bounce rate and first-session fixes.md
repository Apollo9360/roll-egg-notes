# Bounce rate and first-session fixes

Up: [[Retention, Onboarding and Playtime]] · Previous: [[Funnel analysis and playtesting]] · Next: [[Core loop, progression and content depth]]

- The first 60 seconds must be interesting; treat the start like a short video reel.
- Players standing around and leaving within a minute signals confusion.
- Spawn players next to the first objective, facing the right way (owner-recommended).
- Avoid long walks; speed players up during the tutorial or reposition plots.
- Drop players into action or an automatic practice match against bots, as Blade Ball does.
- Auto-equip needed tools; put the item straight into the player's hand, not the hotbar.
- Avoid double proximity prompts; increase prompt range, add click feedback, and support both click and E-prompt.
- Too much UI hurts; about 10 elements on screen is already too many, so cut text and element count.
- Unclear income sources kill playtime; show "+N coins" pop-ups and avoid generic icons.
- Unexplained random deaths read as low quality; explain hazards such as lava.
- Avoid camera switching between first and third person, and use Roblox's default mobile joystick.
- Use familiar patterns from 1k+ CCU games in your genre.
- Expect ad traffic to bounce heavily; 30–50% leaving within seconds and many sub-minute sessions are common.

### FTUE tactics

- Front-load the FTUE (first-time user experience) with a guaranteed strong item or 4–20x luck at the start.
- Script the first rewards, such as fishing catches that go worst, better, then strong.
- Front-load rare items as the hook, with confetti, fireworks and a notification on rare rolls.
- Make rolls fast, but slow down rolls that land big results.
- Auto-equip the best items for the first 10 minutes.
- Guide the first 3 upgrades and make rebirth, sell and shop highly visible.
- At about 2 minutes, or after the tutorial, prompt a free reward such as free levels.
- Add VFX and SFX on every action and on purchases, and a chime in the shop.

### Early progression speed

- Make the first purchase possible within 5–10 seconds; first upgrades should be cheap rather than free.
- Early on, give something meaningful every 30 seconds to 1 minute; later, every 5–10 minutes.
- Keep onboarding goals small; make the first upgrade cost equal the tutorial bonus so it is instant.
- Show a progress ticker such as "$x/125" before an upgrade unlocks.
- Buff income or cut costs for the first 5 minutes; needing 60k currency for the second zone is too slow.
- Add a hidden boost for new players in incremental games.
- Incrementals: progress very fast, then cap for an hour or two while showing big numbers.
- Counterpoint: unlocking too fast can hurt retention, and too slow is safer than too fast.
- Do not let players become overpowered within 2 minutes, such as by spamming income generators.
- Puzzle games need a gradual intro teaching the design language; reference examples are Fez, Rain World and Blue Prince.

### Exit and leave-time tactics

- Show offline earnings on the leave prompt, such as "come back later to claim your money"; many top games do this.
- A one-time leave reward via GuiService.MenuOpened can offer a strong temporary boost, such as a high-tier trainer at x36 power for 2 minutes.
- An "end run" button gives players an excuse to quit; save progress instead.
