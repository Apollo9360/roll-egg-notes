# Core loop, progression and content depth

Up: [[Retention, Onboarding and Playtime]] · Previous: [[Bounce rate and first-session fixes]] · Next: [[D1 and D7 levers]]

- If playtime is bad, the cause is most likely the core loop or the tutorial.
- If onboarding is clear but every stat is bad, the core loop is the problem; change it if D1 is bad enough.
- Without drastically changing the core loop, expect to add only about 1–2 minutes of playtime.
- Balancing in incrementals may matter more than the gameplay itself (low confidence).

### Three layers of a loop

- Short term is the core loop; mid term is progression, what the player works toward (owner-recommended).
- Long term is social flex and status; it is huge for retention (owner-recommended).
- Use glass or transparent walls and visible plots so players can see others' progression.
- Dopamine comes from anticipating a rare result, not receiving it, so build waits for rare outcomes.
- The climax event, such as a rebirth, should feel about twice as satisfying as anything else.
- Add tension; a pure "eat, eat, eat" loop lacks the tension obbies or RNG add to +1 games.
- A self-balancing safe zone, like 99 Nights' campfire, can regulate difficulty automatically.

### Rebirth and long-term progression

- Rebirths are seen as a main reason players keep playing and can lift incremental playtime about 1.5x.
- Rebirths fit games about dominating earlier levels, not every genre, such as PvP card games.
- Aim for at least 15 minutes to the first rebirth; rebirth within 3 minutes means too little content.
- Balancing shortcut: playtest from a fresh start and set the first rebirth price to the cash held at 15 minutes.
- If average playtime is 3 minutes, a rebirth at 5–10 minutes will not help.
- Big games layer plot upgrades, then rebirth upgrades, then ascensions with huge multipliers.
- Merge machines, trait machines and a collection index stretch progression and support D7 (owner-recommended).
- Price upgrades to outscale income, and track time to each upgrade and whether players quit at walls.
- RNG design: one item about 20x rarer than the rest can drive 3-hour chase sessions.
- Over-stimulation is a trap: too many effects early make later rewards feel meaningless.

### Content depth for return visits

- D1 needs a reason to return: something to finish, a stage, a better pet.
- A game beaten in 30 minutes gives no reason to return; one completable in 10 minutes kills retention.
- Incrementals need roughly 8–20 hours of content; 10+ hours is the newer expectation.
- 15–20 hours lets a game run about 10 days without updates after blowing up.
- Ship about a week of playtime of content, then update weekly.
- Once a game has 2–3 days of total playtime, release it and add content if it does well.
- Counterpoint: one +1 game held 25k CCU while beatable in about an hour, so multi-day content is not strictly required.
- Identical playthroughs with no long-term goal end a game within about a week, as reported for one 200k-CCU RNG hit.
- Isolated per-area currencies give no reason to revisit; link them, such as an ascension needing several currencies.
- Horror, story and one-time games struggle with multi-day retention, though some still hold thousands of CCU.
- Update by analytics: add retention features if retention is bad, and depth if playtime is bad.
