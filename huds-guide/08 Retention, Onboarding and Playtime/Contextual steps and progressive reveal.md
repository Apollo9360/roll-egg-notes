# Contextual steps and progressive reveal

Up: [[Retention, Onboarding and Playtime]] · Previous: [[Visual guidance and tutorial UI]] · Next: [[Funnel analysis and playtesting]]

- A proven structure: teach the main mechanic, then the upgrade, then contextual tutorials triggered by proximity to a task or by time.
- Staged example: basic roll-and-earn loop, then a next tutorial unlocked at a money target with a goal bar, then upgrades, then workers.
- Show objects only when they are needed; give systems one by one.
- Hide later systems, such as rebirth billboards, until unlocked; too many systems early lowers playtime.
- Stop forcing the tutorial after the second item, then prompt rebirth once the player can afford it.
- Make secondary mechanics hard to miss; move or teleport an easily missed NPC (non-player character).
- Show daily and playtime rewards on the next join or later, never on join or right after the tutorial.
- "First give, then ask": show invite or event prompts after about a dozen wins, not right after the tutorial.
- A group-join prompt mid-tutorial can stop completion; move it or gate rewards clearly.
- Give tutorial-completion rewards such as timed boosts or potions with big VFX (visual effects) and SFX (sound effects).
- Do not make a core action an upgrade, such as auto-merge; the extra step loses kids.
